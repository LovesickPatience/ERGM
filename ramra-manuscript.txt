Abstract.Multimodal emotional dialogue generation (EDG) has ben-
efited from increasingly powerful fusion architectures, yet many systems
still adopt a text-centric and statically fused multimodal pipeline and fail
to fully exploit audio–visual affective cues whose salience varies across
dialogue contexts. We presentRaMRA, arichness-aware modality-role
assignmentframework that estimates the semantic–sentiment informa-
tiveness of text, audio, and vision and dynamically assigns instance-level
dominant/auxiliary roles. The dominant stream serves as an interaction
hubforrole-conditionedcross-modalattention,whileresponsegeneration
remains anchored in the textual context. Experiments on IEMOCAP and
MELD demonstrate that RaMRA consistently improves fluency, seman-
tic alignment, and emotion consistency over strong baselines, underscor-
ing the importance of adaptive multimodal affect perception for building
emotionally intelligent dialogue agents.

1 Introduction
Dialogue generation has become a core interface for intelligent customer service,
virtual assistants, and everyday human–computer interaction. While neural gen-
erators increasingly master coherence and contextual relevance, they still tend
to under-model affect, resulting in responses that sound fluent yet emotionally
off-target [8]. Emotional dialogue generation (EDG) therefore seeks to produce
replies that are not only contextually appropriate but also aligned with the
speaker’s affective state—a capability at the heart ofemotional intelligencein
human–AI interaction and a prerequisite for truly empathetic dialogue agents.

Multimodal EDG is often motivated by a simple observation: the same words
can convey different emotions under different prosody or facial display. Recent
systems accordingly incorporate audio and vision [3,6], yet many pipelines re-
maintext-centricin a structural sense—text acts as the default backbone, and
non-textstreamsareappendedasauxiliaryrefinementsorglobalconditions.This
design implicitly assumes that extra modalities are uniformly helpful, which is
rarely true in practice: acoustic cues may dominate when wording is neutral but
tone is tense, while facial expressions can be decisive when speech is short or
ambiguous. Even systems built around afixednon-text primary modality inherit
the same limitation—the weighting prior is predetermined rather than inferred
per instance, making them brittle under modality-specific noise, missing cues,
or cross-modal incongruence [22,12,6]. This motivates a key question: “can mul-
timodal EDG explicitly decide which modality should lead the interaction for
each dialogue context, while keeping generation anchored to textual discourse?”
As illustrated in Fig. 1, we argue that multimodal EDG should separate
two functional roles: (i) agrounding channelthat preserves dialogue seman-
tics and controllability (naturally, text), and (ii) aninteraction hubthat adap-
tively routes cross-modal affective evidence to maximize emotional reliability.
To instantiate this principle, we proposeRaMRA1, an informativeness-driven
modality-role assignment and role-conditioned interaction framework within a
decoder-only generation pipeline. For each dialogue context, RaMRA estimates
thesemantic–sentimentinformativenessoftext,audio,andvision,assignsadom-
inant/auxiliary role configuration, and performs cross-modal exchangeasymmet-
rically: the dominant modality serves as the sole query source for cross-modal
attention, while auxiliary streams contribute evidence only through value vec-
tors modulated by their informativeness weights. This role-based asymmetry dis-

Our contributions are threefold:
1.Role-based reformulation.We formulate multimodal EDG asinstance-
level modality-role assignmentand propose a richness-aware criterion that
assigns roles based on semantic–sentiment informativeness.
2.Role-based asymmetric fusion.We introduce role-conditioned asymmet-

redundancy and interference from less informative streams.
3.F ramework & validation.We present RaMRA as a plug-in framework on

its effectiveness on IEMOCAP and MELD through analyses and ablations.
2 Related Work
Emotional Dialogue Generation.Emotional dialogue generation (EDG) aims to
produce responses that are both contextually coherent and emotionally aligned
with the speaker’s affective state. Text-only approaches have progressively en-
richedaffectivegroundingthroughcommonsenseknowledgeinfusion[9],emotion-
sensitive recognition [13], coarse-to-fine cognition–affection alignment [27], and
iterative associative memory [23]. A closely related upstream task, multimodal
emotion recognition in conversations (ERC), provides the perceptual founda-
tion that EDG systems rely on; recent ERC advances include attention-based
correlation-aware fusion [10], adaptive graph learning [11], and graph-spectral
analysis of cross-modal emotion dynamics [1], as surveyed in [16].
Building on these recognition capabilities, multimodal EDG systems have
begun to incorporate audio and visual signals alongside text. Representative ef-
fortsincludetheopen-sourceavatarchatbotEmpathyEar[3],theemotion-guided
LLM pipeline SpeakFromHeart [6], multi-modality fusion in ERGM [18], LLM-
driven empathetic speech generation in EmpathyOmni [12], the text-speech-
vision avatar benchmark AvaMERG [25], and personality-aware multimodal em-
pathetic generation [17]. Despite their progress, these systems share a common
architectural assumption: either text serves as the fixed backbone with non-text
streams appended as auxiliary signals, or a single non-text modality is pre-
designated as the primary channel. Neither design adapts to theinstance-level
variation in modality salience that naturally arises in emotional conversations.

strategies—such as concatenation, element-wise gating, and symmetric cross-
attention—treat all modalities as equally informative, which can amplify noise

4 Experiments
4.1 Datasets
We evaluate onMELD[7] andIEMOCAP[2], two widely used multimodal
emotion benchmarks with aligned text, audio, and visual streams and utterance-
level emotion labels. MELD contains 13,708 utterances from 1,433 dialogues
annotated with 7 emotion categories, and IEMOCAP comprises about 7,433 ut-
terances from 151 dyadic sessions with 6 commonly-used emotion categories. For
MELD, we follow the official train/dev/test split. For IEMOCAP, we adopt the
standard session-based protocol (Sessions 1–4 for training, Session 5 for testing)
and hold out a validation subset from the training sessions for model selection.

While leave-one-session-out cross-validation is the predominant protocol in the
ERC literature [2], we opt for a fixed split primarily for two reasons: (1) it en-
sures a consistent comparison surface with MELD and with prior multimodal
EDG work that also uses fixed train/test partitions [18]; and (2) IEMOCAP
contains only five sessions, making each fold in a leave-one-out scheme a sin-
gle session, which risks high evaluation variance on a test set of approximately
1,500 utterances and complicates checkpoint selection under heterogeneous ses-
sion characteristics.
4.2 Implementation Details
All experiments run on an NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPU. Following ERGM [18],
audio tracks are extracted with FFmpeg (16 kHz), word-aligned via the Montreal
ForcedAligner,andeachutteranceissegmentedintoasynchronizedaudio–visual
clip of 32 frames. We use CLIP ViT-L/14 and HuBERT-Large as frozen visual
and acoustic encoders, respectively. All modalities are projected to a shared
hidden size ofH= 768via linear adapters, and multi-head attention uses 12
heads across all fusion modules.
For RaMRA and the ERGM baseline, both based on a decoder-only GPT-
style backbone (12 layers, 12 heads,H= 768), we fine-tune directly on MELD
and IEMOCAP using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of1×10−5, a
batch size of 16, and 10 training epochs; generation uses nucleus sampling with
p= 0.8.ForLLM-basedsettings,weusetheQwen2.5-OmniThinkermodulewith
a learning rate of1×10−4, feed the concatenated dialogue history as input, keep
modality encoders frozen, and apply LoRA (r= 16,α= 32). Identical decoding
hyperparameters are used across all LLM-based models. Other baselines (CEM,
SEEK, CASE, IAMM) follow the hyperparameters in their original papers, and
all baselines are reproduced using their official codebases. The ERGM baseline is
reproduced without the guidance-text branch (which is not released) and with-
out EmpatheticDialogues pre-training, matching the input pipeline available to
RaMRA.
4.3 Evaluation Metrics
We report the following automatic metrics: perplexity (PPL) for fluency, Dist-
1/Dist-2 [5] for response-level lexical diversity, BERTScore [26] (F1) for semantic
similarity with the ground-truth response, and emotion accuracy (Emo Acc.),
computed by a fixed external emotion classifier—an MLP head trained on each
dataset’s utterance-level emotion labels following the standard ERC protocol—
applied to the generated responses. All models are evaluated by the same ex-
ternal classifier. We acknowledge that unweighted average recall (UAR) is often
preferred over accuracy for imbalanced emotion datasets. However, we report
accuracy for two reasons: (1) all baselines we compare against [9,13,27,23,18]
use accuracy as their primary emotion metric, making it the de facto standard
in multimodal EDG; and (2) the class distributions of both MELD (7 categories,
4,710 train utterances) and IEMOCAP (6 categories, 5,810 train utterances) are

not severely skewed—the most and least frequent classes differ by a factor of
approximately 3–4×, well within ranges where accuracy remains a reliable indi-
cator. We additionally note that because all models are evaluated by thesame
external classifier underidenticalconditions, relative ranking under accuracy
should closely mirror that under UAR.
Human Evaluation.We conduct a pairwise human study on 100 randomly sam-
pled multimodal test instances from MELD and IEMOCAP (pooled for anno-
tation). For each instance, three annotators with NLP research backgrounds
compare two anonymized responses (order randomized) from RaMRA and the
strongest baseline given the same multimodal inputs, and choose the better
one underEmp(empathy/emotion alignment),Coh(contextual coherence),
andFlu(fluency/naturalness). We report RaMRA’s win rate per criterion. For
LLM-based human evaluation, we use the same format-only prompt at inference
for both ERGM(Qwen) and RaMRA(Qwen).

6 Conclusion
We proposeRaMRA, a framework that reframes multimodal EDG as instance-
level modality-role assignment. By identifying a context-specific interaction hub
and performing role-conditioned, asymmetric routing under textual grounding,
RaMRA reduces redundant fusion and emphasizes the most reliable affective
evidence.Thisprincipledrole-basedformulation,togetherwithauxiliaryemotion
supervision, consistently improves emotion-aligned generation on MELD and
IEMOCAP, demonstrating that adaptive multimodal affect perception is key to
building emotionally intelligent dialogue systems.

Limitations and Future Work.While RaMRA demonstrates consistent gains,
several limitations warrant discussion.(1) Dataset scope.Our evaluation
is restricted to two dyadic English conversation datasets (MELD and IEMO-
CAP); validation on larger-scale, multi-party, or multilingual benchmarks would
strengthenthegeneralityclaim.(2) Encoder freezing.WeusefrozenCLIPand
HuBERT encoders, following the setup in ERGM [18]. Fine-tuning the acoustic
encoder could potentially recover more affective signal from prosody, at the cost
of additional computational overhead.(3) Human evaluation scale.Our hu-
man study involves 100 instances and three annotators, which is adequate for
pairwise preference estimation but limits the statistical power for fine-grained
per-category analysis; larger-scale annotations with more annotators would yield
more robust subjective metrics.(4) Modality role analysis.The current work
reportsaggregatedperformancemetricsbutdoesnotprovideabreakdownofhow
often each modality is selected as dominant, or how role assignments correlate
with ground-truth emotion categories.(5) Robustness to degraded inputs.
We do not evaluate performance under missing or noisy modalities, a practical
concern in real-world deployment. Future work will extend RaMRA along these
axes, including per-emotion-category analysis, modality role visualizations, and
evaluation under modality degradation scenarios.
